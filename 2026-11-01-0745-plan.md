# Plan: Did Colfax construction reduce deaths/SBIs beyond the citywide trend?

## Inputs
- Use the CSV `data/ODC_CRIME_TRAFFICACCIDENTS5YR_*.csv` (about 150 MB, point-level crash records with lon/lat). The `.geodatabase` is the same data in another format and is not needed.
- Expected Denver schema (verify on load): FIRST_OCCURRENCE_DATE, INCIDENT_ADDRESS, GEO_LON, GEO_LAT, SERIOUSLY_INJURED, FATALITIES, TOP_TRAFFIC_ACCIDENT_OFFENSE, NEIGHBORHOOD_ID.
- Python env: `.venv` has no data packages. Install via uv: polars, plotnine, scipy, statsmodels, geopandas/shapely/pyproj, pyarrow, jupyter/ipykernel.

## Step 1: Load and validate
- Parse dates. Restrict to Jan 1 through Sep 27 of each year, and drop anything after 2026-09-27.
- Filter to crashes with FATALITIES > 0 or SERIOUSLY_INJURED > 0. Define the unit of analysis as the crash (and also the person count).
- Sanity check against the user's totals (94 deaths and 318 SBIs in 2025). Report any mismatch, e.g. full-year vs Jan-Sep, and whether counts are crashes or persons.
- Investigate missingness: null or zero coordinates, and null dates.

## Step 2: Define spatial zones (projected CRS, EPSG:2232, Colorado Central ftUS)
- **Colfax corridor (treatment):** Colfax Ave between Broadway and Colorado Blvd. Geocode the endpoints from crashes at those intersections (INCIDENT_ADDRESS or intersection fields), or use known coordinates. Select crashes within a buffer of the centerline (e.g. 150 m, with 100 m and 250 m sensitivity), restricted to the segment between the end intersections. Check whether the address string "E COLFAX AVE" agrees with the geometric selection.
- **Alternative routes:** 13th Ave and 14th Ave over the same Broadway to Colorado Blvd extent, using the same buffer approach. The user said "13th and 14th Street"; in Denver these are 13th Ave and 14th Ave east of Broadway, so I will confirm this interpretation.
- **Citywide baseline:** all other Denver crashes, excluding both zones above.

## Step 3: Periods
- Pre-construction: Jan 1 to Sep 27 of 2021 to 2024, minus nothing else. Check which years the 5-year file covers. Because of the Oct 2024 start, Jan to Sep of 2024 is pre-construction.
- Construction: Jan 1 to Sep 27, 2025 and 2026. 2025 and 2026 are both within the construction window (Oct 2024 to Sep 27, 2026), so "construction" applies to both. The "2026 vs 2025 YoY" framing is a separate secondary comparison.
- Note: the user's data cut-off is Sep 27, 2026, and seasonality is handled by matched Jan to Sep windows only.

## Step 4: Formal tests
1. **Primary: difference-in-differences on counts.** Poisson (or negative binomial if overdispersed) GLM with zone x period interaction, with log(exposure) offset if available. Counts per zone x year are small for the corridor, so use exact or conditional methods too. The interaction term's rate ratio with a 95% CI answers whether the Colfax reduction is disproportionate to the citywide one.
2. **Exact conditional test:** compare the Colfax-vs-rest split of crashes pre vs construction with a binomial or Fisher exact test on the 2x2 table (corridor / rest x pre / during).
3. **Spatial test:** a permutation / Monte Carlo test. Randomly place segments of the same length on comparable high-injury streets (the arterial network, or the top-N corridors by baseline SBI density), and compute the pre to during change for each. The Colfax percentile in this null distribution gives an empirical p-value. This controls for regression to the mean, which is a risk since Colfax is a high-crash corridor.
4. **Point pattern check:** kernel density difference (construction minus pre) with a Monte Carlo random-labelling test (Kulldorff-style / relative risk surface via the spatial scan statistic) to see whether the Colfax stretch stands out as a significant cold spot citywide.
5. **Alternative routes:** same DiD for 13th/14th Ave vs baseline. A smaller reduction or an increase relative to baseline supports displacement. Also test Colfax + 13th + 14th combined to check for net corridor effect.
6. Secondary: repeat with all crashes (not just SBI/fatal) and with fatal-only, as power allows.

## Step 5: Diagnostics and caveats
- Overdispersion checks, a pre-trend check (year-by-year rates for the corridor vs rest), and sensitivity to buffer width and the pre-period years used.
- Low-count warning: the corridor probably has only a handful of SBI/deaths per year, so power is limited. Report CIs and the minimum detectable effect, and say so if results are inconclusive.
- Construction could also reduce traffic volume or speeds (a mechanism) but we have no volume data, so this is not a causal identification. Regression to the mean and citywide Vision Zero changes are confounders.
- Multiple tests: designate test 1 as primary and the rest as supportive.
